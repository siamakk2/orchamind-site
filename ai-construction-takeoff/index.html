AI Takeoff

Turn construction plans into an estimate — in minutes, not evenings

The takeoff is the slowest, most error-prone part of bidding: counting fixtures, measuring rooms, transferring numbers to a spreadsheet at 10pm. Orchamind reads the plans and does it for you.

Short answer

Snap photos of the plans on your phone — or upload the PDF — and AI performs the takeoff: rooms, square footage, fixture and outlet counts, systems. It then drafts an itemized estimate with labor and materials split out, lists every assumption it made, and asks about anything the plans don't specify. You verify, apply your rates, and send.

How plan-to-estimate AI works

Upload the plans — a PDF, or up to four photos of the pages taken right at the kitchen table with the homeowner.
AI does the takeoff — it reads rooms, dimensions, square footage, fixture counts, electrical and plumbing elements, and any written notes on the drawings.
It asks instead of guessing — anything the plans don't specify comes back as a short question: fixture grade? ceiling height? is demo included? Answer in plain words and it refines the numbers.
You get an itemized draft — labor and materials split into line items with realistic regional pricing, permits included when the plans imply them.
Review and send — the draft lands in your normal estimate editor. Verify quantities, apply your rates and markup, send it branded, and convert to an invoice in one click when it's accepted.

What the AI reads off your plans

A good estimator reads a set of plans in layers, and the AI works the same way. First the geometry: room dimensions and square footage, wall runs, ceiling heights where noted. Then the counts: fixtures, outlets, switches, windows, doors, panels — the things you'd otherwise tally with a highlighter. Then the systems: what the electrical, plumbing and mechanical scope implies even when it isn't itemized. And finally the fine print: written notes on the drawings, which is where scope surprises usually hide.

From that takeoff it builds the estimate the way you would — quantity times unit price, labor separate from materials — so every line is checkable against the plans, not a mystery lump sum.

The part that matters: it never guesses silently

Plans never tell you everything. The difference between a takeoff you can trust and one that burns you is what happens with the gaps. Orchamind handles them two ways: every assumption is listed in plain English on the draft (“assumed 8 ft ceilings — height not marked on page 2”), and anything that materially changes the price comes back as a clarifying question — up to four, answerable by voice. That's how an experienced estimator works, and it's why the draft is a starting point you can defend rather than a black box.

What it still can't do

Honesty matters here. AI takeoff doesn't replace structural engineering, doesn't see site conditions — the slope, the access, the soil — and doesn't know your crew's production rates or this week's supplier pricing. That's why every AI draft in Orchamind is clearly marked as a draft, and why you review every line before it goes out. The AI removes the counting and the arithmetic; the contractor's judgment makes it a bid.
